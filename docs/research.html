Research

I study ice sheet dynamics, parameter sensitivity and inference, and processes that impact sea level rise contributions, in the Ice Future Research Group. I use the Ice-sheet and Sea-level System Model (ISSM) and the MIT general circulation model (MITgcm) to explore ice–ocean processes controlling ice shelf channelization and test the sensitivity of future ice mass change to future ocean forcings and ice rheology assumptions, to improve projections of West Antarctic ice loss.

Forward model complexity does not produce inherently more reliable results. Deterministic projections inherit the errors and biases of their initial states and parameters, and are only as certain as the inference behind them. Identifiability between the bed, basal friction, and ice viscosity may be weak in critical areas, requiring that inference respect what the model and data can constrain, and treat the remainder as uncertainty. I am interested in developing and applying theoretical and computational methods to make that inference and uncertainty explicit and usable.
